## II. 仔细阅读｜约317词｜5题（建议限时8分钟）
When investigators speak to eyewitnesses, they often begin by asking a simple question: “What did you see?” Yet an answer to that question may contain several different kinds of information. A witness might describe the color of a coat, the sound of a door, and a conclusion about who entered a building. Although these details appear together in a single account, they are not equally reliable.

Psychological research has long distinguished direct observation from interpretation. People do not store perfect recordings of events. Instead, they pay attention to selected details and organize them into a meaningful story. This process is often useful: without interpretation, everyday decisions would become painfully slow. However, it can also create errors when incomplete information is mistaken for a complete explanation.

Consider a person who sees a figure near a doorway and hears the sound of a closing door a few seconds later. The observer may naturally infer that the figure entered the building. That explanation is plausible, but several alternatives remain possible. Someone else could have closed the door, or the figure could have walked past the entrance. The observation itself may be accurate even if the conclusion is wrong.

For this reason, careful investigators avoid asking questions that suggest an expected answer. “Did the man enter the building?” may encourage a witness to agree with an assumption that was never part of the original observation. A more neutral question—“What happened after you saw the figure?”—allows the witness to describe events without being directed toward a particular explanation.

This does not mean that eyewitness accounts should be dismissed. They can provide important information, especially when combined with independent evidence. The goal is not to distrust every witness but to recognize the limits of any single account. By comparing separate sources and taking uncertainty into account, investigators can reach conclusions that are more reliable than those based on first impressions alone.

## IV. 长篇匹配｜5段5题（建议限时6分钟）
**Paragraph A.** A student hears an unfamiliar English word in a lecture. She understands the surrounding idea but cannot recognize the word's sound. Instead of rewriting a definition, she listens to it three times and repeats it in a full sentence. Her main difficulty is sound recognition, not grammar.

**Paragraph B.** Another student knows what the word means when reading, but cannot produce it during a timed essay. She practices translating a familiar Chinese sentence into English without looking at a word list. For her, active recall is more useful than another round of recognition.

**Paragraph C.** A third student answers comprehension questions incorrectly because she treats the author's example as a general rule. Her teacher asks her to underline words such as may, often, and some. This helps her notice that carefully qualified statements are not absolute claims.

**Paragraph D.** A fourth student spends hours memorizing rarely used words but neglects listening and translation. She reorganizes her study time around the exam's actual tasks, keeping a smaller list of expressions that can be reused in several sections.

**Paragraph E.** A final student reads a passage fluently but often chooses an attractive answer unsupported by the text. She starts requiring one sentence of evidence for every reading answer and explains why the other options are misleading.

2. Which student practices distinguishing qualified claims from absolutes?

3. Which student relies on evidence from the passage to justify answers?

4. Which student prioritizes exam transfer instead of accumulating rare words?

5. Which student focuses on the pronunciation of an unfamiliar word?

## V. 写作｜165词参考范文
**题目：** Why should we question seemingly convincing evidence? Write a short essay of 150–200 English words. State your opinion, explain two reasons, and give a practical conclusion.

**写之前的3句骨架：**
1. 立场：Questioning apparently convincing claims is essential for responsible decisions.
2. 理由：Assumptions can be mistaken; the consequences can be significant.
3. 结论：We should verify evidence and take alternative explanations into account.

**参考范文（先写再看）：**

In an age when information spreads rapidly, people are often tempted to accept a convincing story without checking the evidence. I believe that questioning such claims is essential to making responsible decisions.

First, an apparently clear explanation may be based on an inaccurate assumption. For example, someone who observes a person near a door may conclude that the person entered a building. However, the observation alone cannot prove this conclusion. It is therefore important to distinguish facts from interpretations.

Second, unverified information can have significant consequences. In education, public health, and daily life, decisions based on unreliable sources may waste resources or harm others. Taking alternative explanations into account can reduce these risks and encourage independent thinking.

Of course, questioning evidence does not mean refusing to believe anything. Rather, it means comparing reliable sources, examining possible limitations, and remaining willing to revise an initial judgment. In the long run, such an approach allows individuals to draw more reasonable conclusions and participate in society more responsibly.

**不是只背范文**：标注两个独立理由、一个合理举例、一个让步，以及其中的**be based on / distinguish ... from ... / take ... into account / draw conclusions / in the long run**；用新主题（例如AI建议是否可靠）替换例子重新写一段。作文应以准确、清晰为先，避免硬套术语。

## VI. 汉译英｜练习199个汉字（含标点约217字符）
**先做核心句：** 调查中应重视可靠证据，而不能仅凭第一印象作出判断。

**扩展段落（可选，用于提前训练完整段落，不要求第一天全部写完）：**
在日常生活中，人们经常根据亲眼所见或网络信息作出判断。然而，第一印象并不总是可靠的。一个看似合理的解释，可能建立在未经证实的假设之上。为了避免错误判断，我们应当学会区分事实和个人推测，并主动查找不同来源的证据。只有充分考虑相关信息，才能得出更加合理的结论。近年来，越来越多的学校鼓励学生开展独立调查、讨论不同观点，并用可靠证据支持自己的主张。这不仅有助于提高学习效率，也能培养批判性思维，使年轻人在面对复杂问题时作出更加负责任的选择。

**参考译文：**
In everyday life, people often make judgments based on what they have seen or read online. However, first impressions are not always reliable. An apparently reasonable explanation may be based on an unverified assumption. To avoid making mistakes, we should learn to distinguish facts from personal interpretations and actively seek evidence from different sources. Only by taking relevant information into account can we draw more reasonable conclusions. In recent years, an increasing number of schools have encouraged students to conduct independent investigations, discuss different perspectives, and support their claims with reliable evidence. These activities not only help improve learning efficiency but also foster critical thinking, enabling young people to make more responsible choices when faced with complex problems.

**译法拆解：**
- “根据……作出判断”→ make judgments based on ...，其中过去分词based on修饰judgments。
- “并不总是可靠”→ not always reliable（部分否定），不要写are always not reliable。
- “区分事实和个人推测”→ distinguish facts from personal interpretations。
- “只有充分考虑……才能……”→ Only by taking ... into account can we ...（only＋介词短语置句首时，主句部分倒装）。
- “不仅……也……”→ not only ... but also ...；使用两个平行的动词结构。
- “使年轻人……”→ enabling young people to ...，现在分词说明结果，避免中文式逐词硬翻。

## VII. Day01语法两项核心训练
**A. 五大基本句型与并列**
- S+V: The rain stopped.（主谓）
- S+V+O: Lin examined the earth.（主谓宾）
- S+V+P: The ground was smooth.（主系表）
- S+V+IO+DO: The librarian gave Lin a folder.（主谓双宾）
- S+V+O+OC: Nora found the explanation convincing.（主谓宾补）
- 并列：Nora saw the figure, **but** her conclusion was wrong. 两个独立分句用逗号＋and/but连接，不能逗号直接粘两个独立句。

**B. 比较级与部分否定**
- The more carefully we examine a claim, the less likely we are to be deceived.（the + 比较级，the + 比较级）
- In contrast to an actual visitor, a reflection would leave no footprints.（比较对象要在逻辑上相当）
- The most obvious explanation is **not always** correct. ≠ No obvious explanation is correct.（部分否定不能偷换全部否定）
